# Habit Tracker for Climbers

> **Why this idea exists.** It is the B2C control subject for task 3, chosen to exercise the
> consumer lane of the skill system end to end: App Store competitor mapping, 1-star and 3-star
> review mining, ASO scoring, creator-economy fit, freemium conversion, D1/D7/D30 retention, and a
> consumer capture rate. It was not generated from the user's profile and is not a recommendation.

## The Problem

A recreational climber trains inconsistently. They know the things that would make them better —
hangboard twice a week, antagonist work, mobility, sleep — and they do them in bursts, then stop.
Existing climbing apps concentrate on logging what happened at the wall (which routes, what grade)
rather than on building the off-the-wall habits that actually move the grade. General habit
trackers do the opposite: they will happily track "hangboard" as a checkbox but know nothing about
climbing, so they cannot tell the climber whether their week was balanced, whether they are
overloading fingers, or when to deload.

**Emotional trigger:** the frustration of plateauing while training hard, and the low-grade guilt
of knowing exactly which sessions were skipped.

**Audience vocabulary:**
- "stuck at V4 / stuck at 6c"
- "hangboard routine consistency"
- "climbing training plan for intermediates"
- "how do you not skip antagonist training"
- "deload week climbing"

## App Concept

A habit tracker that knows about climbing. The climber picks a small set of recurring commitments
(hangboard, antagonist, mobility, rest days), and the app tracks streaks, warns when finger load is
stacking up without a rest day, and shows a weekly balance view. Logging a climbing session is
one tap; the emphasis is the habits around the session, not the session itself.

**App category:** habit tracker, vertical (sports/fitness)
**Business model:** b2c — the climber pays for it personally, as a consumer subscription.

## Key Features

1. **Climbing-aware habit set** — Preset commitments drawn from climbing training rather than a blank slate.
2. **Finger-load guard** — Flags consecutive high-intensity finger days and prompts a rest day.
3. **Weekly balance view** — Pull, push, mobility, and rest at a glance, so imbalance is visible before it becomes injury.
4. **Streaks with a deload exception** — Rest weeks do not break a streak, removing the usual reason climbers abandon habit apps.
5. **One-tap session log** — Enough to correlate habits with time on the wall, without becoming a logbook app.

## Key Differentiator

Climbing apps log performance; habit apps are sport-agnostic. This sits between them and is the
only one that can say "your fingers have had three hard days in a row, take tomorrow off" — a
statement that requires both habit data and climbing domain knowledge. Whether that wedge is worth
paying for is exactly what this validation is meant to find out.

## Monetization Evidence

Unknown at the outset. Establishing whether climbers pay for training and habit software, and at
what price, is the job of the pricing and competitor steps.

**Monetization validated:** no — to be determined by research.

# Habit Tracking for Climbers — App Store and Category — September 2026

Data observed as of September 2026. B2C control run for task 3; the subject is a spec test rather than a recommendation.

## 1. Executive summary

The two parent markets are healthy. Indoor climbing has more than 6 million annual US participants and gym count is compounding at about 5.5% a year. Habit tracking is a $2.22 billion global market growing at 14.2% CAGR. On those numbers the idea looks reasonable.

The category detail is where it comes apart. Crimpd, made by Lattice Training, publishes a free library of more than 100 structured climbing workouts and charges $4.99 a month for the premium tier. That is a free product from the sport's authority brand sitting exactly where a new entrant would have to price. Above it, LatticePlan at $29-30 a month and Sequence at about $7 a month cover the coaching end.

More decisive than the pricing is what climbers complain about. The documented friction in these apps is technical: no force-gauge integration, no automated periodization, manual load progression, no in-workout grade logging. The assumed problem behind this idea — that climbers know what to do and fail to do it consistently — does not appear as a complaint anywhere in the reviewed material. The gap being targeted is one the research cannot confirm exists as felt pain.

## 2. Competitive structure

| App | Price | Free tier | Documented complaints |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Crimpd** (by Lattice) | Free; Crimpd+ $4.99/mo annual, $9.99/mo monthly | 100+ structured workouts | No force-gauge integration, no built-in periodization, manual scheduling, cannot log grades mid-workout, UI reordering friction |
| **LatticePlan** | £22.99/mo, £124.99/yr (~$29-30/mo) | 7-day trial | Plans set 2.5-hour workouts, rarely under 90 minutes; assessments promised but unshipped as of mid-2026; "still feels half finished" |
| **Sequence** | ~$7/mo | 50 workouts, no card | Steep learning curve; assumes the user understands periodization |
| **KAYA, Stökt** | Freemium logging | Yes | Social and logging tools rather than coaching; not competing on training |
| **General habit trackers** (Streaks, Productive, Habitify) | $2.99-9.99/mo | Varies | Sport-agnostic; know nothing about climbing load |

The structure is a barbell: a free, credible offering at the bottom from the brand climbers already trust, and a premium coaching tier at the top. The middle, where a $4-6 habit product would sit, is occupied by Crimpd's free tier from below and Sequence's $7 tier from above.

## 3. Where the real unmet need sits

Three specific gaps recur across the reviewed comparisons, and none of them is habit formation:

1. **Force-gauge integration.** Neither Crimpd nor Lattice supports Tindeq or equivalent natively; Sequence does it through custom metrics. This is the most consistently named missing feature.
2. **Periodization that adapts.** Crimpd has none, Lattice's weekly check-ins are not test-based and lack full mesocycle structure. Load progression is manual in the market leader.
3. **Time-realistic plans.** The sharpest complaints about the premium product concern workouts of 90 minutes to 2.5 hours that do not fit around a job.

The third is the closest thing to a behavioural complaint, and it is about plan design rather than about tracking. A product that generated genuinely short, adaptive sessions would speak to a documented frustration; a product that tracks whether you did them would not.

## 4. Market size inputs

- More than 6 million annual indoor climbing participants in the US.
- 574 US climbing gyms in 2025, up 4.6% year on year, averaging 5.5% growth over five years; over 600 indoor climbing walls in total.
- Global climbing gym market around $3 billion in 2024, growing near 9.9% CAGR.
- Habit tracking market $2.22 billion global in 2026, US segment roughly $1.34 billion.
- Serious-climber app budget commentary: $20-25 per month total across one coaching app and one logbook.

The participant figure is generous as a starting point: the addressable group is the subset who train deliberately off the wall and would pay for software, which is a small fraction of people who visit a gym.

## 5. Monetization pattern

Freemium dominates habit tracking at 52.3% of revenues, and the climbing incumbent leads with a substantial free tier. Any entrant here is therefore committed to freemium by market convention, competing for conversion against a free product with better brand credibility. Paid-only positioning is available at $2.99-9.99 but would require the product to be visibly better than a free offering from Lattice on day one.

## 6. Risks

1. **Free credible incumbent, severity high.** Crimpd's free tier is the competitor, not its paid tier. Beating free requires a wedge climbers can feel in the first session.
2. **The assumed pain is unevidenced, severity high.** Habit consistency does not appear in the documented complaint set. The idea may be solving a problem its audience does not articulate.
3. **Category budget ceiling, severity medium.** With $20-25 a month described as the realistic total, a new subscription competes against apps climbers already pay for rather than expanding spend.
4. **Small addressable niche, severity medium.** Six million participants narrows sharply to deliberate trainers willing to pay.
5. **Brand authority asymmetry, severity medium.** Lattice is a coaching institution in this sport. A solo entrant has no comparable credibility signal.

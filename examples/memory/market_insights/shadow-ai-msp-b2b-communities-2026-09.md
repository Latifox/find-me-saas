# Shadow AI Discovery for SMB via MSP — B2B Communities — September 2026

Data observed as of September 2026. First execution of the `b2b-communities` research prompt. Sources are buyer-review platforms, MSP trade press, vendor pricing pages, and MSP-facing comparison content.

## 1. Executive summary

The thesis behind this idea — enterprise shadow-AI tooling is priced and architected for 500+ employee organisations, leaving SMBs and their MSPs unserved — is **confirmed on price and contradicted on opportunity**.

Confirmed: the enterprise floor is real and documented. Cyberhaven starts around $40,000/yr with a median recorded contract of $36,444. Obsidian is around $30,000/yr, Reco around $25,000/yr. Nudge Security, the most down-market of the credible vendors, still charges a $750/month flat fee below 150 users. A 60-seat company cannot buy any of these sensibly.

Contradicted: the gap is not empty and is not waiting. It is being closed from three directions simultaneously.

1. **From above, by Microsoft.** Purview is now included in M365 Business Premium, at roughly $22/user/month equivalent. For SMBs already on that SKU the capability arrives inside a licence they have already bought. It will not be as good, and that will not matter to a buyer whose motivation is passing an audit question.
2. **From the side, by purpose-built competitors.** ShadowLock sells per-device monthly with multi-tenant architecture and is actively publishing MSP-targeted content. Lavawall publishes an MSP partner pricing model with volume tiers. Both occupy the precise position this idea proposes to take.
3. **From inside the channel, by incumbents.** Cynomi, Barracuda and Keeper Security are publishing shadow-AI guidance for MSPs during 2026. These vendors already hold the partner agreements, the billing relationship and the quarterly business reviews with the same MSPs a new entrant would have to cold-approach.

The demand signal is excellent. The competitive position is not, and the channel is the hardest part rather than the easy part.

## 2. The buyer and the trigger

The end pain is well evidenced. Unauthorised AI use ran at 68% of enterprise employees in early 2026, up from 41% in 2023, and adoption does not slow when IT says no. Forty-eight percent of employees have put non-public company information into AI tools, including customer data, financials, legal documents and personnel records. One organisation in five has already had a breach linked to shadow AI. Only 18% have a formal AI security policy.

Critically, the buying conversation is **responsive**: clients raise it themselves, prompted by SOC 2 audits, HIPAA reviews and cyber-insurance renewals. That is the healthiest possible demand shape, because it arrives with a deadline and a budget line attached rather than requiring evangelism.

But note who the buyer is in this idea. It is not the SMB. It is the MSP, and the MSP buys on a completely different basis.

## 3. The channel is the problem, and it is structural

MSP vendor selection is documented as an institutional process, not a relationship one. Published selection criteria for security vendors include SOC 2 Type II certification, ISO 27001, current penetration-test reports, SSO and MFA support, audit logging, partner training and enablement material, pre- and post-sales support, marketing resources and incentives, and a partner advisory board. Evaluation explicitly covers "maturity of the company and its products" and vendor financials.

Every one of those is a gate a bootstrapped solo vendor fails at the outset. SOC 2 Type II alone is a six-to-twelve-month process with real cost, and it is table stakes for selling security software to a buyer whose own clients ask them for SOC 2 evidence. An MSP that resells a tool inherits responsibility for it; that is why the bar is where it is.

This is not a case of a channel that is slow to warm. It is a channel with an explicit qualification checklist that a new solo vendor cannot satisfy, competing against vendors who already satisfy it and already have the relationship.

The category's review corpus is almost empty — Harmonic Security shows 4.8 stars from two G2 reviews — which cuts both ways. There is no incumbent with overwhelming social proof, and equally there is no evidence that any of these products has found a repeatable buyer.

## 5. Market size inputs

- US MSP population: roughly **40,000-50,000** firms, spanning solo operators to firms with hundreds of staff.
- Serviceable share is much smaller. The MSPs who buy a new security SKU, have enough SMB clients to make per-device economics work, and are not already committed to a platform vendor are a minority of that population.
- Revealed billing unit for the channel is **per device per month**, confirmed by both ShadowLock and the MSP-facing comparison content, which explicitly argues per-user pricing creates "strange economics" for multi-device organisations.

## 6. Risks to be honest about

1. **Bundling risk, severity high.** Microsoft Purview inside M365 Business Premium is the classic way an SMB security category dies. Good-enough and already paid for beats better and separately invoiced.
2. **Channel qualification, severity high.** SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001, pen-test reports and a partner programme are documented selection criteria. A bootstrapped vendor cannot present them, and cannot buy them cheaply.
3. **Position already occupied, severity high.** ShadowLock and Lavawall are not adjacent; they are in the seat, with published MSP pricing.
4. **Incumbent encroachment, severity medium-high.** Cynomi, Barracuda and Keeper reach the same MSPs through partner programmes built over years.
5. **No proof of repeatable purchase, severity medium.** Two G2 reviews across the leading pure-play means nobody has demonstrated a durable buyer yet. Early can be indistinguishable from wrong.
